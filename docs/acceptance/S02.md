## Behavior evidence

Domain/filesystem tests cover all offered read windows, aware start-time validation,
server timestamp visibility, bounded UTF-8 retention, monotonic identities, mark
expiry, clipboard limits, mode-0600 exclusive saves, existing-file/symlink refusal
and draining filesystem work through cancellation and late failure.

The 16 log-view Pilot cases use actual owned HTTP sessions and resource watches.
They cover 40×12/100×30 windows, literal hostile markup/Unicode and redacted output,
regular/init selection, current/previous errors, time/window selection, quiet-open
and empty EOF states, mouse and Vim scrolling, page/half-page keys, reception
pause versus viewport follow, horizontal offsets, wrapping, timestamps, search,
clear/marks/fullscreen/copy/save, resize and preserved table state on return.
They also cover paused cleanup, context change under a child picker, same-name
UID replacement and fatal read/render/save errors through application cleanup.

One stream delivers 15,000 lines: retention/layout caches settle at 5,000,
10,000 dropped lines are reported and rendering occurs in fewer than 100 batches.
Head 1000 closes after the oldest first thousand lines. Another controllable
stream receives more than 5,000 new lines while the operator reads: surviving
viewport identity is preserved, an evicted anchor clamps to the oldest retained
line, marks expire and reception remains independent of follow. These are bounded
behavior checks, not a sustained maximum-load/RSS benchmark; Q03 remains open.

Qualification exposed two lifecycle/layout failures and now guards them: dismiss
cancels tasks before removing widgets, and a virtual-size change synchronizes
scrollbar bounds even when outer panel geometry stays unchanged. The initial
follow and mouse-scroll regression exercises this behavior across all interpreters.

Source and fresh-install PTYs send actual l/p/g/j/k/G/search/copy/help/Esc/Ctrl+Q
bytes, resize the terminal, exercise an unavailable previous instance and verify
cleanup/restoration without leaking fixture credentials or changing kubeconfig.
They wait for a modal to close before sending another key, matching actual screen
transitions rather than accidentally composing an Alt sequence.
